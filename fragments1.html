Take heart

Another word pressed deep upon me this day: take heart. I have pondered those who have spoken it in ages past. Oft are they the very ones who stand amid the same shadow and sorrow, yet they bid another rise. Their own path is dim, their own burden no lighter—yet hope burns within them so fiercely it borders on holy madness. My heart ached to behold such strength: that a man, though cast down, should yet summon courage for his brother. I long to bear that word not lightly upon my tongue, but within my chest. When a spirit is bowed and heavy, I would not speak it as an empty comfort. Rather, I pray that when the hour comes, I shall first command mine own soul: **Take heart.** Let me taste the courage before I offer it. Let me gather the scattered embers within, that I may reflect a steadied spirit before I seek to raise another from the dust.

Stronghold

It was a morning where I went forth to walk in the highway, and mine eyes fell upon a sign bearing the word Stronghold. At the first I recked it little, and in the folly of my spirit even made light of my mother’s estate within that company.

Yet as I journeyed on, the word clave in twain within my thoughts,

strong and hold, might and keeping. And there rose within me the sacred saying in Psalms 27:1 “The Lord is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear? The Lord is the stronghold of my life; of whom shall I be afraid?” Then was my heart stilled upon the road, and I perceived the weight and sweetness of that simple name. For a stronghold is no mere wall of stone, but a fastness wherein the soul is kept from dread. Gladness pressed upon my breast, and clarity like morning light was granted unto me, as though a veil had been drawn aside. A revelation. Thus upon the common way was I taught a quiet mystery.
